Direct Cold Call

The goal: A good conversation and a properly qualified custom demo. Speak naturally, and do not rush someone onto the calendar.

1
Reach the decision maker
"Hey, is this [Business Name]?"
"This is [Your Name] with Summit Results Agency. Are you the owner?"
2
Open the conversation
"The reason I'm calling is we're helping [Industry] businesses make sure customer calls don't get lost when everyone's busy or the office is closed. When someone calls during a job or in the evening, does it ever go unanswered or to voicemail?"
Lead with the problem, not the AI. Let them explain what happens.
3
Understand what happens now
"Roughly how many calls would you say you miss on a normal day or week?"
"And what usually happens with those callers after that? Do they leave a voicemail, call back, or is it kind of hit or miss?"
"Who normally handles the phone when you guys are available?"
If they rarely miss calls, briefly ask about evenings or busy periods. If there is no real need, move on.
4
Explain the solution
"Got it. That's exactly what we help with. We set up an AI receptionist that can step in when your team can't answer. It can talk to the customer, answer common questions, collect their information, and either help get them booked or make sure your team has everything they need to follow up."
"The main idea is just making sure those calls don't disappear into voicemail."
Pause and let them react. Ask whether that would be useful for their situation.
5
Check value and fit
"Roughly what is an average new customer or job worth to you?"
"So if a few more of those callers actually became customers each month, would that make a difference for you?"
Use their numbers, not invented guarantees. The calculator below can help if you need it.

Talk about price before booking
"Just so you know, the AI receptionist is $397 a month. That includes the custom setup around your business and unlimited call volume."
"If we showed you it could handle your calls the way you need, is that a price you could realistically consider?"
Wait for an actual answer. A prospect who says the price will never work is not ready for a custom build.
7
Offer the custom demo
"What I'd like to do is have our team put together a version specifically for your business, so you can hear how it would answer your customers. Then we'd walk you through it together and you can decide if it makes sense. Would you be open to that?"
This is a custom demo, not an obligation to buy. Check that the person who makes the decision will attend.
"Would you be the person making that decision, or should we have someone else join us?"
8
Book and set expectations
"Perfect. Would [day/time] or [other day/time] work better?"
"We'll look at your business and prepare that demo before we meet, so you'll get to hear something built for you. If it does what you need, we can talk through the next steps then."
"And if anything comes up and you need to reschedule, that's completely fine. Just give us a heads-up so we aren't waiting on each other. Sound good?"
Book while they are on the call. Repeat the date, time, and time zone. Verify their phone and email. Record the problem, price response, decision maker, and any custom requirements.

Before the calendar

Appointment Qualification

A booked demo is only valuable if the right person shows up, understands the offer, and has a real reason to consider it. We're building something custom for them, so take this seriously.

The simple test: If the demo works the way they need and they like what they hear, is there a real possibility they would become a client? They don't have to promise to buy. But they should be genuinely open to it.

The qualification checklist

Decision maker: You're speaking with the owner or someone who can make the decision. If someone else must approve, they're invited to the demo.
Actual problem: Calls go unanswered, the team is busy, after-hours calls are missed, or their current call handling needs work. You understand what happens today.
Business value: You have an idea of call volume, typical customer value, and why fixing it might matter. Don't promise specific results.
Clear product: They know we're talking about an AI receptionist that answers calls, helps callers, captures information, and can support scheduling or routing.
Price discussed: You've clearly told them it's $397 per month for the standard AI receptionist, and they've said they could realistically consider that if it works for them.
Custom demo understood: They know we'll prepare a version for their own business and walk through it with them. It's not just a generic sales pitch.
Genuine interest: They want to solve the problem and would consider moving forward if the demo does what they need.
Timing: You know whether they're considering making a change soon or just gathering information for later.
Real meeting: They chose a specific date and time, understand how they'll join, and their phone number and email are correct.
Rescheduling commitment: You've asked them to let us know if anything changes, and they agreed.
Useful handoff: The CRM and booking notes include their problem, current setup, decision maker, price response, business details, questions, and anything the custom demo should show.

What should stop you from booking?

Book the custom demo

The right person is involved, there's a plausible problem, they know the price, and they are genuinely willing to look at a solution. They agree to a real time and know a custom demo will be prepared.

Don't force the booking

They can't consider the price, won't involve the buyer, have no relevant need, or only agree to a meeting to end the conversation. Clarify the concern, offer the public live demo if useful, and leave a sensible follow-up instead of wasting everyone's time.

Never qualify someone by pressure. An honest "not right now" is better than a no-show. If they aren't ready, record why and when it makes sense to follow up.

Notes the demo team should actually receive

Price discussed, what they said about $397, and buying timeline
What they want to hear in the demo: booking, intake, FAQ, transfer, hours, language, or something else
Any objection, special integration, or promise requiring approval

Objection Handling

Do not memorize clever rebuttals. Find out what the objection actually means, respond to that, and move the conversation forward. On the first call, that's usually a qualified demo. After the demo, it's a decision.

1Diagnose

"What makes you say that?"

2Understand

Listen for the real concern.

3Reframe

Use their situation and numbers.

4Confirm

"Does that address it?"

5Next step

Book the demo or ask for the sale.

"Just send me some information."

"Of course. Just so I send you something useful, is the main thing you're curious about how it would answer your calls, or is it more about pricing?"

If there's a real problem and interest, offer to show a custom example instead of sending a generic pitch and hoping they reply.

"We can actually put together a quick example for your business. That way you can hear it yourself and see if it would work. Would you be open to a 15-minute demo?"

"I don't have time for a demo."

"No problem. That's actually why I wanted to schedule something rather than take up your time now. Would early next week be easier, or is this just not a priority at the moment?"

If it's not a priority or they can't realistically consider the price, don't force a calendar slot.

"Can I just try it first?"

"Absolutely. We have a live example you can call. And if it seems useful, we can build one around your business and walk you through that together."

"$397 is too expensive."

"Got it. What makes you say that?"

Do not defend the price until you know what they mean. Often the real answer is that they do not believe they miss enough calls, they do not see enough customer value, or they are comparing the number to a cheaper alternative.

"That's fair. Let's see if it even makes sense. You said you're missing about [X] calls and an average customer is worth about $[Y]. If solving even part of that creates more than $397 in value, then it makes sense. If it doesn't, I wouldn't tell you to buy it either."
"It's also about $13 a day for unlimited 24/7 call handling. The bigger question is whether the calls it helps capture are worth more than that."

"We don't miss enough calls."

"That may be true. About how many would you say actually go unanswered in a day or week?"

Use the real number. If the economics are weak, do not manufacture urgency. If even a few missed calls are valuable, work through that together.

"Another company is cheaper."

"Absolutely, there are cheaper options. What are they including at that price?"

Compare scope, not logos. Ask about call limits, per-minute charges, simultaneous calls, customization, scheduling, integrations, and what happens when volume increases.

"If their setup does everything you need at that price, that may be a good option. I just want to make sure we're comparing the same thing."

"I don't want AI answering my calls."

"I understand. It doesn't have to replace the way you answer calls now. Your team can still answer like normal. The AI can mainly step in when nobody is available, so the alternative is usually AI versus voicemail, not AI versus your staff."

"I need to think about it."

"Of course. What specifically do you feel like you need to think through?"

Do not accept a vague stall without understanding it. If the concern is price, product, timing, partner approval, or trust, deal with the real issue. If they genuinely need time, schedule the next conversation with yourself.

"I need to talk to my partner."

"Makes sense. Is there anything you think they'll want to know before making the decision?"

Answer what you can, then invite both decision makers to the custom demo. The person who must approve the purchase should hear the presentation.

When price is the only blocker

Handle the objection and establish value first. If price is genuinely the only issue, ask for the exact monthly amount they would accept.

"If I can get $[exact amount] approved, will you move forward today?" Confirm a clear yes, then contact management with the exact amount and their commitment.

Management may approve, counter, or decline. Keep the conversation with the prospect and never promise unapproved pricing.

Know when to stop. Objection handling is for real opportunities with a solvable concern. It is not a contest to argue an uninterested person into submission.

Reschedules and no-shows

They tell you they need to move it

"No worries, thanks for letting me know. Would [day/time] work instead?"

Move the event, notify the team, and keep the original context in the notes.

They missed the demo

"Hey [Name], looks like we missed each other today. We had the custom demo ready for you. If you're still interested, let me know a better time and we can reschedule."

Don't mark an unconfirmed or missed meeting as held. Record the outcome honestly.

Daily operating checklist

  • Commit at least 6 hours to each full working day.
  • Make at least 125 outbound dials during each full working day.
  • Work toward 3 properly qualified demos a day, not just three calendar bookings.
  • Qualify the decision maker, problem, price, and intent before booking.
  • Explain that we're preparing a custom demo and ask the prospect to let us know if plans change.
  • Confirm upcoming demos about an hour before their scheduled time.
  • Keep every active prospect's CRM record, booking notes, and next step current.
  • Communicate blockers or schedule changes promptly. Do not go silent.
  • Submit the Daily Activity Form at the end of every working day. Report actual results, even when you miss the target.
Quality isn't optional. Three bookings don't count as three qualified appointments if the owners have no interest, haven't heard the price, or don't know why they're meeting. Track attendance and outcomes, learn from no-shows, and ask for coaching when you need it.
